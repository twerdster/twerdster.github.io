Overview

HandNet pairs real depth images with magnetic measurements of the position and orientation of five fingertips and the palm. The accompanying paper introduces DeROT: estimate global hand orientation, normalize in-plane rotation, and detect fingertips in the normalized image. This supplementary viewer exposes the released data and the geometry of ground-truth normalization.

The viewer uses stored samples from the original archives. It does not run a trained network or report new benchmark results.

Calibration and display conventions

The native camera coordinate system is +X right, +Y down, +Z away, with positions in millimetres and zero-based image coordinates. Depth reconstruction uses the original camera calibration:

X = (u − cx) Z / fx     Y = (v − cy) Z / fy

fₓ = 240.30773969018747   fᵧ = 240.07488537862218
cₓ = 178.29264757987775   cᵧ = 117.9581118815394

The 3D display flips Y and Z together to preserve handedness. Numeric measurements retain the source coordinate system. Sensor axes use the columns of each recorded 3 × 3 orientation matrix. Heatmap peaks can differ from projected sensor centers, particularly in transformed archives.

Ground-truth orientation normalization

Method in the paper ↗

From orientation regression to a canonical image

The paper estimates a global hand orientation and uses it to choose an in-plane rotation before fingertip detection. Removing rotational variability is intended to reduce the appearance space that the detector must learn.

  1. Measure or estimate orientation. HandNet provides magnetic orientation annotations; the published DeROT method learns to predict orientation from depth.
  2. Normalize image rotation. An orientation-dependent rule chooses a canonical in-plane view, with the thumb base on a consistent side.
  3. Detect and transform back. Fingertip detections in the normalized view are mapped to the original camera frame.
Scope of this demonstration.

The controls use the recorded palm orientation as ground truth. They demonstrate the geometric normalization convention from the original code. No orientation network or fingertip detector is executed.

Reference implementation: derotate.m and rotateData.m.

Provenance and display subset

Stored examples include their source archive member, session and frame indices, and MAT SHA-256. The local sample manifest records calibration, units, sampling and display preprocessing. The archive manifest records original byte sizes and checksums.

Historical frame counts

The paper reports 212,928 unique hand poses. The released standard archives contain 214,971 MAT frames. This viewer reports the distributed archive counts and does not claim to reconcile the discrepancy.
